Lesson 6. The Wonderful Wizard of Oz.

Story introduction.

The Wonderful Wizard of Oz is a timeless classic. It mainly tells the story of the fearless and clever girl, Dorothy, who is swept up by a powerful cyclone, and transported to the mysterious land of Oz, together with her unforgettable companions, she sets off on a journey to meet the mysterious great wizard in the sparkling emerald city. Let's get ready for the exciting adventures, fights against wicked witches, and important lessons about friendship and bravery.

Character introduction.

Dorothy. Dorothy is a kindhearted girl who finds herself in the magical land of Oz after a cyclone carries her away from her home in Kansas. Throughout her journey, she remains strong and caring, making friends and facing challenges with bravery.

Toto. Toto is a little black dog who brings joy and companionship to Dorothy's life, as Dorothy's faithful and loyal companion, he accompanies her on her adventures in Oz, and stands by her side through thick and thin.

The scarecrow. The Scarecrow is a friendly and lovable character who is in search of a brain. Despite his lack of intelligence, he often offers wise ideas, he becomes one of Dorothy's closest friends and joins her on the journey to meet the Great Wizard.

The Tin Woodman. The Tin Woodman is a kindhearted character who is searching for a heart. He is gentle, kind, and always willing to lend a helping hand. The Tin Woodman becomes a faithful friend to Dorothy and the others, showing his loyalty and affection.

The Cowardly Lion. Despite his fears, the Cowardly Lion possesses a heart full of courage. He joins Dorothy and her friends on their journey to seek courage from the great wizard. Along the way, he discovers his own inner strength and learns to overcome his fears.

The Good Witch of the North and Glinda. The Good Witch of the North is the 1st magical character Dorothy encounters in Oz. She provides guidance and assistance directing Dorothy toward the Emerald City. Glinda, the good witch of the South, becomes a very important figure, she helps Dorothy and her friends on their journey by offering wisdom and support.

The Great Wizard of Oz. The mysterious ruler of the Emerald City, the Great Wizard of Oz, is seen as all powerful and highly respected. Dorothy and her friends ask for his help to fulfill their wishes.

The wonderful Wizard of Oz.

Chapter one. The cyclone.

Dorothy lived in the midst of the vast Kansas prairies, along with her Uncle Henry, a farmer, and Aunt Em, his wife. Their house was modest, with a big bed in one corner for Uncle Henry and Aunt Em, and a small bed in another corner for Dorothy. There was no attic or cellar, except for a tiny hole in the ground, known as a cyclone cellar, where they could seek shelter in case a powerful whirlwind capable of destroying anything in its path, came. The cellar could be entered through a trap door in the middle of the floor, leading to a small and dark space below.

Aunt Em had grown thin and gaunt, seldom smiling anymore. Uncle Henry worked tirelessly from morning till night, not knowing the feeling of joy. It was Toto, Dorothy's little black dog, brought laughter, and joy to her life, brightening her days and keeping her spirits up. Toto and Dorothy played together all day, and she cherished him dearly.

On this particular day. Uncle Henry sat on the doorstep, his gaze fixed anxiously upon the unusually gray sky. Aunt Em was busy washing the dishes. Suddenly, Uncle Henry stood up and called out to his wife, Em, there's a cyclone coming. I'll go check on the livestock. With that, he hurried toward the sheds where the cows and horses were kept.

Aunt Em stopped her work and rushed to the door. A single glance showed there was danger. Quick, Dorothy, she screamed, run to the cellar. Dorothy managed to grab Toto and followed her aunt. However, halfway across the room. A fierce gust of wind roared, causing the house to tremble violently. Losing her balance, Dorothy tumbled to the floor.

Then something extraordinary happened. The house spun around 2 or 3 times, and slowly went up into the air. Dorothy felt like she was high up in a balloon. The meeting of the north and south winds directly above the house, made it the center of the cyclone, though the air within a cyclone is typically calm, the tremendous force of the wind on all sides of the house lifted it higher and higher, despite the dark and the scary sound of the wind, Dorothy realized she was floating steadily.

As time went by, Dorothy became less scared. She moved across the shaky floor, and laid down on her bed. Toto came too, and snuggled next to her. Despite the house's constant movement and the wild wind. Dorothy soon closed her eyes, and fell into a deep and peaceful sleep.

Chapter 2. The council with the Munchkins.

When Dorothy woke up, she sat up suddenly in bed, and noticed that the house had stopped moving, sunlight streamed into the room, filling it with brightness, jumping out of bed, she opened the door, and her eyes widened with astonishment as she took in the incredible sights before her.

Some very strange people that she had never seen before were coming toward her. Three were men, and one was a woman all dressed in peculiar attire. They wore hats with bells on them that made a sweet tinkling sound when they moved. The men wore blue outfits that matched their hats, and their shiny boots had a deep blue cuff. The woman had a white hat and a long dress with pleats flowing from her shoulders, sparkling stars were sprinkled over her gown, shimmering like diamonds in the sunlight. While the bearded men appeared to be about Uncle Henry's age. The woman was undoubtedly much older. Her face was lined with wrinkles. Her hair was nearly white, and she walked stiffly.

The old woman walked up to Dorothy, bowed nicely and spoke in a pleasant voice. Welcome, great magic lady, to the Munchkins place. We're very thankful to you for beating the bad witch of the east and saving us from her mean rule.

Dorothy hesitated before answering. You are very kind, but I think there must be some mistake. I haven't killed anyone.

Your house certainly did, replied the woman, chuckling, and that's exactly the same thing. Look, she added, pointing to the corner of the house. You can still see her two feet under a wood beam.

Dorothy looked and gasped in fear. Underneath the corner of the large beam. She saw two feet sticking out, wearing silver shoes with pointed toes.

Curious, Dorothy asked. Who was she?

She was the wicked witch of the East, a wicked sorceress, replied the woman. She made the munchkins work very hard for her for many years. Now that they're free? They're thankful to you.

Dorothy gazed sadly at the lifeless witch. Suddenly, the feet vanished entirely, leaving behind only the silver shoes.

She was so old. The woman explained, that she dried up quickly under the sun. That's the end of her, but the silver shoes are now yours, and you should wear them. Then she reached down, picked up the shoes, and after dusting them off, handed them to Dorothy.

The Witch of the East treasured those silver shoes, the woman added, and they hold some magical power, though we never found out what it was.

Dorothy accepted the shoes, and looked at the woman. Who are you? She asked.

I'm the witch of the North, replied the woman, but I'm a kindhearted witch, beloved by the people. If I had the same power as the wicked witch who once ruled here, I would have freed the munchkins myself.

But I thought all witches and wizards were wicked. remarked Dorothy.

Oh no, the woman explained. In the land of Oz, we only had four witches, two are nice, living in the north and the south. I'm one of the nice ones, the other two witches from the east and the west were mean, but you defeated the mean one from the east, so now we only have one mean witch left in the west. And we also have a great wizard named Oz. He's a good wizard, the strongest of all, and lives in the Emerald City.

Dorothy then asked, how can I return home?

To return home, you must make your way to the Emerald City. Perhaps Oz will help you.

Where is this city? Dorothy asked.

It lies exactly at the center of the country, and it's ruled by Oz, the great wizard I mentioned earlier.

How can I reach it? Dorothy inquired.

You must travel on foot, the path to the Emerald City is paved with yellow bricks, so you cannot miss it. However, it will be a long journey through a land that can be both pleasant and dangerous, but I will use my magic to keep you safe. When you get to Oz, don't be afraid. Just tell him your story and ask for his help.

Goodbye. The Witch of the North said, bidding farewell to Dorothy.

Chapter 3. How Dorothy saved the scarecrow.

Then Dorothy went back to the house and began making preparations for the journey to the Emerald City. She looked down at her feet, and noticed how old and worn her shoes were. Then she took off her old leather shoes, and tried on the silver ones, which fitted her as well, as if they had been made for her. Come along, Toto, she said, we will go to the Emerald City, and ask the great Oz how to get back to Kansas again.

With Toto following closely, she began her journey. It did not take her long to find the road paved with yellow bricks. Within a short time, she was walking briskly toward the Emerald City. By evening, Dorothy arrived at the home of one of the richest munchkins in the land, whose name was Boq. Boq led her into the house, where he gave her a room with a pretty bed in it. Toto curled up on the blue rug beside her.

The next morning, she ate a hearty breakfast. How far is it to the Emerald City, the girl asked?

I don't know, answered Bock gravely, for I have never been there. It's better for people to keep away from Oz, unless they have business with him. But it is a long way to the Emerald City, and it will take you many days. The country here is rich and pleasant. But you must pass through rough and dangerous places before you reach the end of your journey.

This worried Dorothy a little, but she knew that only the great Oz could help her get back to Kansas, so she bravely determined not to turn back. She bid her friends goodbye, and again started along the road of yellow bricks.

After walking long a while, Dorothy reached a large cornfield. In the distance, she spotted a scarecrow sitting on a pole, guarding the corn from birds. She leaned her chin upon her hand, and gazed thoughtfully at the scarecrow. Its head was a small sack, stuffed with straw, with eyes, a nose, and a mouth painted on it to represent a face.

When Dorothy looked earnestly into the queer, painted face of the Scarecrow, she was surprised to see one of the eyes slowly wink at her. Who are you? asked the Scarecrow, when he had stretched himself and yawned. And where are you going?

My name is Dorothy, said the girl, and I'm going to the Emerald City to ask the Great Oz to send me back to Kansas.

Do you think, he asked, if I go to the Emerald City with you, that Oz would give me some brains?

I cannot tell, she returned, but you may come with me if you like. If Oz will not give you any brains, you will be no worse off than you are now.

Then Dorothy helped him over the fence. They walked back to the road and started along the path of yellow bricks for the Emerald City.

Toward evening, they came to a great forest where the trees grew so big and close together that their branches met over the road of yellow bricks.

The Scarecrow led her through the trees until they reached a cottage, which Dorothy entered and found a bed of dried leaves in one corner. She lay down at once, and with Toto beside her, fell into a sound sleep. The scarecrow, who was never tired, stood up in another corner and patiently waited until morning came.

Chapter 4. The rescue of the Tin Woodman.

Early the next day, as she finished her meal and prepared to go back to the yellow brick road, Dorothy heard a deep groan nearby. Then she discovered something shining in a ray of sunshine that fell between the trees. She ran to the place and then spotted it. It was a man made entirely of tin. His head and arms and legs were jointed upon his body, but he stood perfectly motionless, as if he could not move at all.

Dorothy looked at him in amazement.

Who are you? asked Dorothy.

I am the Tin Woodman, answered the Tin Man. How did you happen to be here?

We are on our way to the Emerald City to see the Great Oz, she answered.

Why do you wish to see Oz? he asked.

I want him to send me back to Kansas, and the Scarecrow wants him to put a few brains into his head, she replied.

The Tin Woodman appeared to think deeply for a moment. Then he said, Do you think if I go to the Emerald City with you that Oz would give me a heart?

Come along, said the Scarecrow heartily, and Dorothy added that she would be pleased to have his company. So the Tin Woodman held his axe, and then they passed through the forest until they came to the road paved with yellow bricks.

Chapter 5. The Cowardly Lion.

Just as Dorothy and her companions entered the thick woods, a terrible roar shook the leaves. Then, a great lion bounded into the road, little Toto, now that he had an enemy to face, ran barking toward the lion, and the great beast had opened his mouth to bite the dog, when Dorothy cried out, don't you dare to bite Toto, you ought to be ashamed of yourself, a big beast like you, to bite a poor little dog. You are nothing but a big coward.

I know it, said the lion, hanging his head in shame. I've always known it. It is my great sorrow, and makes my life very unhappy. I don't mean to harm you, but where are you going?

We are on our way to the Emerald City to see the Great Oz? Dorothy answered. And I'm going to ask him to send Toto and me back to Kansas, she added.

I'm going to the Great Oz to ask him to give me some brains remarked the Scarecrow.

And I'm going to ask him to give me a heart, said the Tin Woodman.

Do you think the Cowardly Lion asked, if I go to the Emerald City with you, that Oz would give me courage?

Come along, said the Tin Woodman heartily, and Dorothy added that she would be pleased to have his company. So once more, the little company set off on the journey, the lion walking proudly beside Dorothy.

Chapter 6. The Journey to the Great Oz.

They had to spend the night outdoors under a big tree in the forest because there were no houses nearby. In the morning, Dorothy washed her face in a small brook, and then they continued their journey toward the Emerald City. They soon heard strange noises in the depths of the forest, and the lion whispered to them that the kalidas lived there.

What are the Kalidahs? Asked the girl Dorothy.

They are huge beasts with bodies like bears, and heads like tigers, replied the lion, and with claws so long and sharp that they could tear me in two as easily as I could kill Toto. I'm terribly afraid of the Kalidahs.

Suddenly, they came to a gulf across the road, but this one was so broad and deep that the lion knew at once he could not leap across it.

Look, there's a big tree by the ditch. If the Tin Woodman can chop it down in a way that it falls on the other side, we can easily walk across, suggest the Scarecrow.

The woodman set to work at once, and his ax was so sharp that the tree was soon chopped nearly through. Then the lion put his strong front legs against the tree, and pushed with all his might. And slowly, the big tree tipped and fell with a crash across the ditch. With its top branches on the other side. They had just started to cross this queer bridge, when a sharp growl made them all look up. To their horror, they saw 2 great beasts running toward them with bodies like bears and heads like tigers.

They are the Kalidahs, said the Cowardly Lion, beginning to tremble.

Quick, cried the Scarecrow. Let's cross over.

So Dorothy went first, holding Toto in her arms. The Tin Woodman followed, and the Scarecrow came next, the lion, the last to cross over, turned to see what the two Kalidahs would do next, without stopping an instant, the fierce beasts, also began to cross the tree. And the lion said to Dorothy, We are lost, for they will surely tear us to pieces with their sharp claws, but stand close behind me, and I will fight them as long as I am alive.

Wait a minute, called the Scarecrow. He had been thinking about what was best to be done, and now he asked the woodman to chop away the end of the tree that rested on their side of the ditch. The Tin Woodman began to use his axe at once, and just as the two Kalidahs were nearly across, the tree fell with a crash into the gulf. The falling branch carried the ugly, snarling brutes with it. Both were dashed to pieces on the sharp rocks at the bottom. It was not long before they reached the road of yellow bricks, and turned again toward the Emerald City, where the great Oz lived.

Chapter 7. The wonderful city of Oz.

The next morning, as soon as the sun was up, they started on their way, and soon saw a beautiful green glow in the sky just before them, that must be the Emerald City, said Dorothy, as they walked on, the green glow became brighter and brighter, and it seemed that at last, they were nearing the end of their travels.

It was afternoon before they came to the great wall that surrounded the city. The wall was high, thick, and of a bright green color. In front of them, and at the end of the road of yellow bricks, was a big gate. Then the big gate swung slowly opened, and before them stood a little man.

We came here to see the Great Oz, said Dorothy. The man was so surprised at this answer, that he sat down to think it over.

I'm the guardian of the gates, and since you demand to see the Great Oz, I must take you to his palace. But 1st you must put on the spectacles, said the little man.

The guardian of the gates led them through the streets until they came to a big building, exactly in the middle of the city, which was the palace of Oz, the great wizard. There was a soldier before the door.

Please make yourselves comfortable while I go to the door of the throne room, and tell Oz you are here, the soldier said.

When, at last, he came back, Dorothy asked, have you seen Oz?

Oh no, returned the soldier, I have never seen him. But I gave him your message.

After a while, Dorothy and her friends started for the throne room of the Great Oz. They opened a little door, and what interested Dorothy most was the big throne of green marble that stood in the middle of the room. It was shaped like a chair. In the center of the chair was an enormous head, without a body to support it, or any arms or legs whatsoever. There was no hair upon his head, but it had eyes and a nose and a mouth, and was much bigger than the head of the biggest giant.

Then, the mouth moved. I am Oz, the great and terrible. Who are you? and why do you seek me?

I'm Dorothy. I wish you to send me and Toto back to Kansas, where my Aunt M and Uncle Henry are, she answered earnestly.

I'm only a scarecrow stuffed with straw. Therefore, I have no brains, and I come to you praying that you will put brains in my head instead of straw, said the Scarecrow.

I'm a woodman and made of tin. Therefore, I have no heart and cannot love. I pray you to give me a heart so that I may be the same as other men, said the woodman.

I'm a cowardly lion. Afraid of everything. I come to you to beg that you give me courage, so that in reality, I may become the king of beasts, as men call me, said the lion.

Well, said the head, I will give you my answer, you have no right to expect me to do anything for you, unless you do something for me in return. In this country, everyone must pay for everything he gets. If you wish me to use my magic power to help you, then you must do something for me first. Help me, and I will help you.

What can we do for you? Asked the girl.

Kill the wicked witch of the West, answered Oz. Dorothy, I will send you and your dog back to Kansas, Scarecrow, I will bestow upon you a great many brains, enough to make you the wisest man in all the land of Oz, Woodman, I will then give you the biggest, kindest, and most loving heart in all the land of Oz. Mayan, I will give you courage, so you will become the king of beasts in all the land of Oz. Now go, and do not ask to see me again until you have done your task.

Sorrowfully, they left the throne room. What shall we do now? asked Dorothy, sadly.

There is only one thing we can do, the lion replied. We will travel to the land of the winkies, seek out the wicked witch, and destroy her.

Chapter 8. The search for the wicked witch.

Next morning, they turned toward the west, leaving the Emerald City far behind. Unknown to them, the Wicked Witch of the West had spotted Dorothy and her friends in her country, and she was furious. Being a powerful and wicked witch, she quickly had a plan of action.

In her cupboard, the wicked witch kept a golden cap adorned with diamonds and rubies. This cap possessed a special charm. By wearing it, the owner could summon the winged monkeys, and give them three commands. However, the cap's power could only be used three times in total. The Wicked Witch had already used it twice. So she hesitated to use it again, until she had used all her other means.

Standing on her left foot, the witch chanted the charm, Epe, Pepe, Kake, instantly, the charm took effect, and the winged monkeys appeared before her. She ordered them to go and capture the strangers who had entered her land and bring them to her. The monkeys swiftly obeyed and carried Dorothy and her companions to the witch's castle.

When the wicked witch looked down and saw Dorothy's silver shoes, she became filled with fear, she knew the shoes possessed a powerful magic. However, Dorothy never took off the shoes except at night, and during baths because the witch was terrified of water, and therefore unable to seize the shoes, determined to get them, the witch made a cunning plan.

Hiding her true intentions, the witch secretly placed an iron bar on the kitchen floor, unseen by anyone. As Dorothy crossed the floor, she stumbled over the hidden bar and lost one of her silver shoes. The Wicked Witch was pleased with her successful trick. Now that she had one of the shoes in her possession. Dorothy was very upset about losing her shoe, and asked for it back, the witch, refusing to give it back, claimed it as her own, Dorothy's anger grew, and she grabbed a nearby bucket of water, soaking the witch from head to foot, to Dorothy's astonishment, the wicked woman let out a cry of fear, and began to shrink and dissolve. In a short time, she had melted away completely.

Next, Dorothy went to the witch's cupboard to get food for their trip, and found a golden cap. She tried it on and it fit just right. She didn't know it was magical. So she decided to wear her silver shoes, her sun hat, and put the cap in her basket.

We have to go back to Oz and remind him of his promise, Dorothy said. Let's start our trip to the Emerald City.

Chapter 9. The discovery of Oz, the terrible. The following day, while taking a break, Dorothy imagined the golden cap. She discovered instructions written inside that she believed to be a magical spell, reading the directions attentively, she put the cap on her head, and recited the charm, Ep, pay, cock, K, calling forth the winged monkeys. The monkeys appeared, making noise and flapping their wings, prepared to follow her orders.

Dorothy expressed their desire to go to the Emerald City, and the monkey king assured them that they would be carried there.

Within a while, the monkeys carried them to the city's gate, and rang the bell. The guardian of the gates, whom they had met before, opened the gate for them.

They walked into the throne room and saw a little old man with a bald head and a face full of wrinkles, surprised by Dorothy and her companions, the man said he was Oz, the great and terrible.

Dorothy asked about his magic, and Oz explained that he was just a common man. The Scarecrow asked for brains, but Oz replied that he didn't need them as he was constantly learning and getting smarter. The lion said he needed courage, but Oz told him he was already brave, just needed to believe in himself. The Tin Woodman wanted a heart, but Oz said having a heart could make him sad sometimes, and the woodman was lucky.

Dorothy then asked how she could return to Kansas. Oz said that he had no idea, and suggested she call the winged monkeys to carry her across the desert. The Tin Woodman reminded her of the golden cap, and Dorothy realized it was a brilliant idea. She fetched the cap, called the winged monkeys, and requested to be flown to Kansas. However, the monkey king regretfully refused her. He explained that they couldn't leave their home, because they were tied to it.

Feeling discouraged. Dorothy decided to ask the soldier for help. The soldier suggested seeking guidance from Glinda, the powerful witch of the South, who ruled over the Quadlings. Dorothy inquired about the route to Glinda's castle, and the soldier warned them that the journey southward was dangerous. The path was filled with wild animals, and unfriendly inhabitants. The Quadlings themselves rarely traveled to the Emerald City because of these hazards.

Wishing to meet Glinda, they chose to start the next morning, everyone prepared well for the trip, knowing it would be hard.

Chapter 10. The Country of the Quadlings.

In the morning, they kept going and came to a dense forest. They made it through the rest of the woods safely, and found a big hill with many rocks in front of them. The Scarecrow admitted the difficulty of the climb, but insisted they should go on. He led the way, followed by the others, as they neared the 1st rock, a rough voice shouted for them to keep back.

Curious, the scarecrow asked who the voice belonged to, a head popped up over the rock, and the person declared that they owned the hill and forbade anyone from crossing it. However, the Scarecrow insisted that they crossed the hill since they were heading to the country of the Quadlings.

The Tin Woodman suggested calling the winged monkeys, reminding Dorothy that she still had the right to command them once more. Dorothy agreed, and she put on the golden cap and recited the magic words. The winged monkeys promptly appeared before her, awaiting her orders.

When they asked what she wanted, Dorothy simply told the winged monkeys to carry them over the hill to the country of the quadlings. The monkeys promptly lifted Dorothy, her companions, and Toto in their arms, and flew them safely over the hill, setting them down in the beautiful country of the Quadlings. The monkey king then informed Dorothy, that this would be their last time being summoned and bid them farewell, wishing them luck.

Dorothy and her companions walked through fields and over pretty bridges until they got to a beautiful castle.

Chapter 11. Glinda the Good Witch.

They were taken to a room in the castle to freshen up before being brought into a large room, where which Glinda sat on a throne of rubies, Glinda looked beautiful and young, with red hair and kind blue eyes. Dorothy told her story to the witch, sharing how she had come to the Land of Oz, and the adventures she had with her friends. She expressed her strong desire to return to Kansas.

Glinda assured Dorothy that she could help her go back home, but in return, Dorothy needed to give her the golden cap. Dorothy happily agreed, since she had no use for the cap anymore.

Glinda then turned to the scarecrow and asked about his plans once Dorothy had left. The Scarecrow revealed his intention to return to the Emerald City, where he was well liked by the people. His only concern was how to cross the hill.

Glinda assured him that he would use the golden cap, to command the winged monkeys, to carry him safely to the gates of the Emerald City.

Next, Glinda asked the Tin Woodman what he planned to do after Dorothy left. The Tin Woodman said he wanted to go back to the Winkies country, where they liked him, and wanted him to be their ruler. Glinda promised to ask the winged monkeys to take him safely to the land of the Winkies.

Then, Glinda asked the lion what he would do when Dorothy went home. The lion said he wanted to go back to the big forest, where the animals there treated him like a king. Glinda promised to ask the winged monkeys to take him back to his favorite forest.

After using the last commands of the golden cap, Glinda said thanks and goodbye to Dorothy and her companions. Dorothy was happy when she found out that her silver shoes could take her back to Aunt Em in Kansas. All she had to do was tap the heels together 3 times, and tell them to take her home.

With tears and hugs, Dorothy bid farewell to her beloved companions. Glinda came down from her throne to give Dorothea goodbye kiss, and Dorothy expressed her gratitude for the kindness Glinda had shown to her friends and herself.

Taking Toto in her arms, Dorothy said her final goodbye, and tapped her heels 3 times, commanding the shoes to take her home to Aunt Em. Instantly, she whirled through the air, and found herself sitting on the vast Kansas prairie. Dorothy stood up, realizing she had lost her silver shoes during the flight. They were lost forever in the desert.

Chapter 12. Home again.

Aunt Em had just stepped out of the house to tend to the cabbages when she glanced up and saw Dorothy rushing toward her. Overwhelmed with joy. She hugged the little girl tightly, showering her with kisses. My dear child, where on earth did you come from? She exclaimed.

Dorothy, with a serious expression, replied, I've come from the Land of Oz, Aunt Em, and look, Toto is here too. Oh, Aunt Em, I'm so glad to be home.
