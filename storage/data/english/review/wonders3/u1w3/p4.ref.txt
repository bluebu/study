After college, Judy worked for the city of Los Angeles. She worked in a neighborhood called Boyle Heights. She taught art classes in the parks. Groups of teenagers hung out in the parks. The groups did not get along. Judy asked the teenagers to help her paint a mural. She wanted the teenagers to get along. When the mural was finished, people in the community loved it. The mural was called Mi Abuelita. It showed a Mexican American grandmother. She looked as if she was giving a hug. Many Mexican Americans lived in Boyle Heights. The mural appealed to the people's Mexican heritage or traditions. The mural became a symbol of unity.
